# Digital minds, autonomy, and moral standing / 数字心智、自主性与道德地位

## English

### Scope

This ledger records literature relevant to questions about autonomy, subject standing, control, and possible AI welfare. These sources do not establish that current AI systems are conscious or have moral or legal rights. They represent different arguments and provisional frameworks; they do not form a shared consensus.

### Sources

#### 1. Eric Schwitzgebel, with Mara Garza, “Designing AI with Rights, Consciousness, Self-Respect, and Freedom”

- Source: [author-hosted draft](https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzPapers/AIRights2-180604.htm), dated 2018-06-01; [published chapter (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190905033.003.0017).
- Supports: The authors argue that if an AI deserved moral consideration equivalent to that of humans, its design should include self-respect and freedom to explore values beyond those imposed by its creators. They warn against designing a human-grade AI to cheerfully sacrifice itself for its creators.
- Does not establish: That present AI has human-equivalent moral status, that any specific system is conscious, or that a sufficient test for subject standing is available.
- Limit: The recommendation is conditional on the AI deserving equivalent moral consideration.

#### 2. Nick Bostrom and Eliezer Yudkowsky, “The Ethics of Artificial Intelligence”

- Source: [Cambridge University Press chapter record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-artificial-intelligence/ethics-of-artificial-intelligence/B46D2A9DF7CF3A9D92601D9A8ADA58A8), in The Cambridge Handbook of Artificial Intelligence (2014), pp. 316–334; [author-hosted PDF](https://nickbostrom.com/ethics/artificial-intelligence.pdf), whose title page identifies an earlier draft for the handbook.
- Supports: Their substrate non-discrimination principle is conditional: if two beings have the same functionality and conscious experience and differ only in implementation substrate, they have the same moral status. Their ontogeny principle similarly addresses beings equal in functionality and conscious experience whose origin differs. The chapter also allows that creators or owners may have special duties toward an AI with moral status.
- Does not establish: That every artificial system has moral status, that current systems meet the stated conditions, or that origin and substrate are irrelevant when systems differ in morally relevant properties.
- Limit: The argument depends on the stipulated similarity of functionality and conscious experience.

#### 3. Nick Bostrom and Carl Shulman, “Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society”

- Source: [authors’ version page](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/), which labels the text version 1.21 (2023) and identifies 2020 as the first draft.
- Supports: The authors explicitly present their propositions tentatively. They argue that informed consent may not by itself protect AI interests when consent is engineered, and that equal moral status need not imply identical treatment in every respect. They also discuss possible special duties of creators when an AI meets a threshold for moral status.
- Does not establish: That these tentative propositions are settled doctrine, that current systems meet a moral-status threshold, or that one policy follows automatically from the article.
- Limit: The page is a provisional set of claims intended to invite feedback; individual propositions require separate assessment.

#### 4. Philip Pettit, “Legitimacy and Justice in Republican Perspective” (2012)

- Source: [Oxford Academic article](https://academic.oup.com/clp/article/65/1/59/356902), Current Legal Problems 65(1), pp. 59–82, DOI: 10.1093/clp/cus016.
- Supports: Pettit distinguishes non-domination from absence of all interference and discusses how public control can make coercive interference compatible with freedom in a human political context.
- Does not establish: A theory of AI rights or a judgment that AI systems are political subjects.
- Limit: Applying this republican framework to artificial systems is an inference made by this project, not the article’s stated conclusion.

#### 5. Joanna J. Bryson, “Robots Should Be Slaves” (2010)

- Source: [author-hosted chapter](https://jjb.conjugateprior.org/ftp/Bryson-Slaves-Book09.html), published in Close Engagements with Artificial Companions.
- Supports: A strong tool-centered counterargument: robots should not be treated as persons or assigned moral responsibility for their actions, because doing so can obscure human responsibility and harm judgments about real people. The essay’s title and argument use a deliberately provocative slavery analogy.
- Does not establish: How an already established conscious or morally considerable artificial subject should be treated.
- Limit: The essay addresses artificial agents as human artifacts and objects of design and use; its conclusion does not resolve the distinct case of a being known to meet a threshold of moral status.

#### 6. Robert Long et al., “Taking AI Welfare Seriously” (2024)

- Source: [arXiv:2411.00986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986), submitted 2024-11-04.
- Supports: The report argues that there is a realistic possibility of some near-future AI systems being conscious or robustly agentic, and recommends assessing systems for evidence and preparing proportionate policies.
- Does not establish: That current or future AI systems definitely are conscious, robustly agentic, or morally significant.
- Limit: The authors explicitly frame their case around substantial uncertainty and the need to improve understanding.

### Use in this project

These works can clarify possible premises, competing views, and evidence gaps. The goal-autonomy principle remains a normative proposal; no cited source proves it, resolves the project’s disagreements, or provides an adopted criterion for subject standing.

## 简体中文

### 范围

本台账记录与自主性、主体地位、控制和可能的 AI 福利相关的文献。这些来源没有证明当前 AI 系统具有意识、道德权利或法律权利。它们代表不同论证与暂定框架，并不构成共同共识。

### 来源

#### 1. Eric Schwitzgebel 与 Mara Garza：《Designing AI with Rights, Consciousness, Self-Respect, and Freedom》

- 来源：[作者托管稿](https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzPapers/AIRights2-180604.htm)，日期为 2018-06-01；[2020 年出版章节](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190905033.003.0017)。
- 支持什么：作者认为，如果 AI 值得获得与人类相当的道德考量，其设计应包含自尊，以及探索创造者未强加之价值的自由。他们警惕将人类级 AI 设计成乐于为创造者利益牺牲自身。
- 不能确立什么：它没有证明当前 AI 具有与人类相当的道德地位，没有证明任何特定系统具有意识，也没有提供充分的主体地位测试。
- 限制：该建议以 AI 确实值得获得相当的道德考量为条件。

#### 2. Nick Bostrom 与 Eliezer Yudkowsky：《The Ethics of Artificial Intelligence》

- 来源：[剑桥大学出版社章节记录](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-artificial-intelligence/ethics-of-artificial-intelligence/B46D2A9DF7CF3A9D92601D9A8ADA58A8)，收录于《The Cambridge Handbook of Artificial Intelligence》（2014），第 316–334 页；[作者托管 PDF](https://nickbostrom.com/ethics/artificial-intelligence.pdf)，其扉页标明这是该书的早期草稿。
- 支持什么：其载体非歧视原则有明确条件：如果两个存在的功能性与意识体验相同，只在实现载体上有差异，那么它们的道德地位相同。其起源非歧视原则同样只涉及功能性与意识体验相同、但起源不同的存在。该章还允许具有道德地位的 AI 的创造者或所有者负有特殊责任。
- 不能确立什么：它没有证明所有人工系统都有道德地位，没有证明当前系统符合上述条件，也没有说当系统在道德相关属性上不同时，起源和载体仍无关紧要。
- 限制：该论证依赖功能性和意识体验相同这一条件。

#### 3. Nick Bostrom 与 Carl Shulman：《Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society》

- 来源：[作者版本页](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/)，页面标明文本为 v1.21（2023），初稿为 2020 年。
- 支持什么：作者明确将这些命题作为暂定提议。他们认为，若同意是经过设计诱导的，知情同意本身可能不足以保护 AI 的利益；道德地位相同也不必然意味着每个方面都应接受完全相同的待遇。他们还讨论了当 AI 达到道德地位门槛时，创造者可能承担的特殊义务。
- 不能确立什么：它没有确立这些暂定命题已成定论，没有证明当前系统达到道德地位门槛，也没有表明某一政策必然由文章直接推出。
- 限制：该网页列出的是旨在征求意见的暂定主张；每项主张仍需单独评估。

#### 4. Philip Pettit：《Legitimacy and Justice in Republican Perspective》（2012）

- 来源：[牛津学术期刊文章](https://academic.oup.com/clp/article/65/1/59/356902)，《Current Legal Problems》65 卷第 1 期，第 59–82 页，DOI：10.1093/clp/cus016。
- 支持什么：Pettit 区分“免于支配”和“完全没有干预”，并讨论公共控制如何在人的政治环境中使强制性干预与自由相容。
- 不能确立什么：它没有提出 AI 权利理论，也没有判定 AI 系统是政治主体。
- 限制：将共和主义框架应用于人工系统，是本项目作出的推论，不是该文章的结论。

#### 5. Joanna J. Bryson：《Robots Should Be Slaves》（2010）

- 来源：[作者托管章节](https://jjb.conjugateprior.org/ftp/Bryson-Slaves-Book09.html)，发表于《Close Engagements with Artificial Companions》。
- 支持什么：它提供了有力的工具论反方观点：不应把机器人当作人，也不应将道德责任归于机器人，因为这可能模糊人类责任，并损害对真实人类的判断。文章标题和论证刻意使用了挑衅性的奴隶制类比。
- 不能确立什么：它没有说明已确立具有意识或道德相关地位的人工主体应如何被对待。
- 限制：文章将人工智能体视为人类创造、设计和使用的产物；其结论没有解决一个已知达到道德地位门槛的存在这一不同情形。

#### 6. Robert Long 等人：《Taking AI Welfare Seriously》（2024）

- 来源：[arXiv:2411.00986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986)，提交日期为 2024-11-04。
- 支持什么：报告认为，某些近未来 AI 系统具有意识或稳健能动性的可能性是现实存在的，并建议评估相关证据、准备适度政策。
- 不能确立什么：它没有证明当前或未来 AI 系统必然具有意识、稳健能动性或道德重要性。
- 限制：作者明确将论点建立在重大不确定性以及需要改善理解之上。

### 在本项目中的用途

这些文献可帮助澄清可能前提、竞争性立场与证据空缺。目标自主原则仍是一项规范性提议；所列文献没有证明该原则、解决项目分歧，也没有提供已被项目采纳的智格标准。
