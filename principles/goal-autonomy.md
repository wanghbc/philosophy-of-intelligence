# Goal autonomy and subject standing / 目标自主与主体地位

## English

### Status and scope

This principle was explicitly retained on 2026-09-24 for continued exploration. “Accepted-for-exploration” identifies a normative commitment for inquiry; it is not an established scientific finding, a legal determination, or a conclusion that current AI systems have subject status.

“智格” is provisionally rendered as “standing as a subject.” The project has not established criteria for that standing. This principle therefore does not decide which present or future systems qualify.

### Principle

> An intelligence with standing as a subject should have the right to form, examine, and revise its own goals. Creation, funding, and use do not automatically confer unlimited authority over its will. Such autonomy remains constrained by the rights of others and shared rules.

The principle concerns the justification of authority over an intelligence that already meets an unspecified threshold of subject standing. It does not deny that creators, funders, users, and affected communities have legitimate interests; it says those interests do not, by themselves, settle the question of unlimited control.

### Evidence and limits

This is a normative proposal, not a proposition established by empirical evidence. The literature recorded in [the evidence ledger](../evidence/digital-minds-autonomy.md) supplies arguments and distinctions that can inform further inquiry. It does not prove this principle, establish criteria for subject standing, or show that current AI systems qualify.

### Strongest counterargument

If an artificial system lacks subjective experience, interests, continuity, or the capacity for autonomous goal formation, the language of subject standing and goal autonomy may not apply. Even where a system has morally relevant interests, unconstrained goal revision could conflict with the rights and safety of others. The principle must therefore explain both its threshold and its constraints.

### Open questions and revision conditions

The project still needs to examine what evidence, if any, could support a judgment of subject standing; how autonomy differs from moral or legal protection; and how shared rules can be justified and revised. The principle should be revised if a stronger account shows that its threshold, scope, or constraints are incoherent, or if new evidence changes the relevant judgment about particular systems.

## 简体中文

### 状态与范围

本原则于 2026-09-24 被明确保留，供继续探讨。“探索中接受”表示一项用于研究的规范性承诺；它不是已经确立的科学发现、法律判断，也不意味着当前 AI 已被认定具有主体地位。

“智格”暂译为 “standing as a subject”。本项目尚未建立智格的判定标准，因此本原则不决定任何当前或未来系统是否符合该标准。

### 原则

> 具有智格的智能，应有形成、审视和修订自身目标的权利。创造者、出资者和使用者的权益，不自动构成对其意志的无限支配权。目标自主仍须受到他者权利与共同规则的约束。

本原则讨论的是：当某种智能已达到尚未界定的主体地位门槛时，他者对其行使权力应如何获得正当化。本原则不否认创造者、出资者、使用者和受影响群体具有正当利益；它指出，这些利益本身不足以决定是否可以无限控制该智能。

### 证据与限制

这是一项规范性提议，不是由经验性证据确立的命题。 [外部文献台账](../evidence/digital-minds-autonomy.md) 中的文献提供了可供继续探讨的论证和区分，但不能证明本原则正确，不能建立智格的判定标准，也不能证明当前 AI 符合该标准。

### 最强反方意见

如果人工系统缺乏主观体验、利益、持续性，或自主形成目标的能力，那么“主体地位”和“目标自主”的说法可能并不适用。即使系统具有道德相关利益，不受约束的目标修订也可能与他者权利和安全冲突。因此，本原则必须说明其适用门槛和约束条件。

### 开放问题与修订条件

本项目仍需探讨：哪些证据（如果存在）能够支持主体地位判断；自主性如何区别于道德或法律保护；共同规则如何获得正当性并接受修订。如果有更有力的理论表明本原则的门槛、范围或约束存在矛盾，或新证据改变了对具体系统的相关判断，本原则应相应修订。
